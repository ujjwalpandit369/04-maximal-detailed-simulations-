Beyond Schwarzschild: The Inner World of Black Holes

The Schwarzschild Penrose diagram revealed a surprisingly rich structure, including a parallel universe. However, it had a fatal flaw for any would-be interstellar traveler: an unavoidable, all-crushing spacelike singularity. Adding electric charge (the Reissner-Nordström solution) or rotation (the Kerr solution) to a black hole dramatically changes this internal structure. The singularity becomes "timelike," meaning it's a place in space that can, in principle, be avoided. Furthermore, a new, second horizon appears inside the first one: the Cauchy horizon. Crossing this inner horizon leads not to oblivion, but to a new "universe" within the black hole, which then connects to another black hole, and so on, in an infinite chain. This simulation visualizes this incredible, repeating structure, allowing you to explore how the properties of the black hole open up these theoretical pathways to other regions of spacetime.

The Two Horizons: Event Horizon and Cauchy Horizon

When a black hole has charge or rotation, the single event horizon of the Schwarzschild case splits into two.

  • The Outer Horizon (Event Horizon): This is the familiar point of no return. Once you cross this from the outside, you can no longer escape back to the universe you came from. However, unlike in the Schwarzschild case, crossing it doesn't doom you to hit a singularity. Instead, you enter a new region of spacetime between the two horizons.
  • The Inner Horizon (Cauchy Horizon): This second horizon is a more bizarre boundary. It is the boundary of the region of spacetime that can be predicted from the initial conditions in the exterior universe. Crossing the Cauchy horizon takes you into a new universe, but it comes at a price. According to the theory, any incoming signals from the outside universe would be infinitely blueshifted and amplified at this boundary, likely creating a new, chaotic singularity. This "mass inflation instability" suggests that while the mathematical solution is beautiful, the interior of a realistic black hole might be far more violent.

The Timelike Singularity

Perhaps the most profound change is the nature of the singularity. In the Schwarzschild case, the singularity is "spacelike"—it lies in the future of every observer who enters the black hole. It's a moment in time, not a place in space, and is thus unavoidable. In the Reissner-Nordström and Kerr solutions, the singularity is "timelike." It is a place in space (located at r=0) which an observer can cleverly avoid by maneuvering their spaceship. By avoiding the singularity, an observer can pass through the black hole's core and emerge through a white hole region into a new, asymptotically flat universe.
