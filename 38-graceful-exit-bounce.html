Beginner's Guide: The Cosmic Bounce

The standard Big Bang theory starts from a singularity—a point of infinite density and temperature where our laws of physics break down. The Pre-Big Bang scenario aims to resolve this. We saw in the previous simulation that the universe contracts towards this point. So what happens at `t=0`? In string theory, the `a -> 0` singularity is not a true end-point. Thanks to symmetries like T-duality, the universe doesn't crush to a point but enters a new "stringy phase" where our normal ideas of space and time dissolve. It then "bounces" and emerges as the hot, expanding universe we know.

This simulation visualizes this entire history. The plot on the left shows the size of the universe (the scale factor `a`) as a function of time `t`. Time `t=0` is the moment of the bounce. You can see the universe contracting from the distant past (`t < 0`), reaching a minimum (but non-zero!) size at the bounce, and then entering the expanding post-bang phase (`t > 0`) that we inhabit. The plot on the right shows the Hubble parameter `H`, the universe's expansion rate. You can see it is negative during contraction, passes through the bounce, and becomes positive during expansion. This "graceful exit" from a contracting phase to an expanding one is a key feature of the model and provides a potential solution to the singularity problem.

Core Theory: The Graceful Exit Problem

A major challenge for any pre-big bang or bouncing cosmology is the "graceful exit problem." How does the universe smoothly transition from the contracting phase (described by one set of physical laws) to the expanding phase (described by another)? The Pre-Big Bang scenario posits that as the universe contracts and the dilaton `φ` grows, the gravitational coupling becomes strong, and the universe enters a new, non-perturbative "string phase" when the curvature reaches the string scale.

The physics of this phase is not fully understood, but it is hypothesized that new stringy degrees of freedom become relevant, causing the universe to "bounce." This simulation models this bounce phenomenologically. We stitch together three phases:

  1. Pre-Bang (`t < -t_s`): The contracting solution `a(t) ∝ (-t)^(-1/2)`.
  2. Post-Bang (`t > +t_s`): The expanding, radiation-dominated solution `a(t) ∝ t^(1/2)`.
  3. Bounce (`-t_s < t < +t_s`): A smooth interpolation between the two phases. To ensure the Hubble parameter `H = (da/dt)/a` is well-behaved, we can interpolate `a(t)²` as a simple function (e.g., linear or quadratic) between its values at the matching points `-t_s` and `+t_s`. This ensures a smooth transition.

The plot of the Hubble parameter `H(t)` is particularly revealing. In the pre-bang phase, `H` is negative and its magnitude grows. In the post-bang phase, `H` is positive and its magnitude decreases. The bounce must connect a state of `H -> -∞` to `H -> +∞`. This is the "singularity" from the perspective of the 4D effective field theory, but in the underlying string theory, it is expected to be a smooth, well-defined physical process. Successfully modeling this graceful exit and ensuring that the density perturbations generated in the pre-bang phase are correctly transmitted through the bounce to match our observations is the primary challenge and goal of this entire cosmological program.

Advanced Details: Branch-Changing Cosmology and the Dilaton

The "graceful exit" is more than just a smooth bounce; it's a "branch change." The pre-bang solution `a ∝ (-t)^h` with `h < 0` and the post-bang solution `a ∝ t^h` with `h > 0` are distinct solutions to the cosmological equations. The stringy bounce phase connects these two previously disconnected branches of solutions. This is a profound concept: the physics of the very early universe may have involved transitions between different cosmological solutions.

What about the dilaton `φ`? In the previous simulation, we saw `φ` evolve towards strong coupling (`e^φ -> ∞`) as `t -> 0`. During the bounce, the dilaton must also be stabilized. The full theory suggests that higher-order corrections to the effective action, which become important at the string scale, modify the potential for the dilaton. These corrections can create a local minimum for the dilaton potential, effectively "freezing" the dilaton at a constant value `φ_0` during the post-bang phase. This is crucial, as a massless, evolving dilaton in the present day is ruled out by observations (it would violate the equivalence principle). Therefore, a successful graceful exit must not only solve the singularity problem but also fix the value of the dilaton, thereby determining the fundamental constants of nature, like the gravitational constant `G_N`, in our current universe.

Interactive Elements Guide

This simulation allows you to explore the dynamics of the graceful exit bounce.

  • Bounce Duration (ts): This slider controls the duration of the bounce phase, `[-t_s, +t_s]`. A smaller `t_s` corresponds to a sharper, more abrupt bounce, while a larger `t_s` represents a more gradual transition. Observe how the shape of the scale factor `a(t)` and the Hubble parameter `H(t)` change. A very small `t_s` makes the bounce look almost like a cusp, closer to the singular behavior of the standard model, while a larger `t_s` smooths it out significantly.
  • Pre-Bang Power Law (hpre): This controls the exponent of the scale factor in the contracting phase, `a(t) ∝ (-t)^(h_pre)`. The default value is `-0.5`, corresponding to the string-inspired solution. You can explore other values to see how different contraction histories would affect the setup for the bounce. Note that `h_pre` must be negative.
  • Post-Bang Power Law (hpost): This controls the exponent of the scale factor in the expanding phase, `a(t) ∝ t^(h_post)`. The default is `0.5`, corresponding to a radiation-dominated universe. You could set it to `2/3` to model a matter-dominated universe or `1` for a stiff-fluid dominated universe.
  • Show Dilaton: This checkbox toggles the display of the dilaton field `φ(t)`. The simulation shows the dilaton growing logarithmically in the pre-bang phase (`φ ∝ -ln(-t)`), reaching a peak at the bounce, and then stabilizing to a constant value in the post-bang phase. This illustrates the crucial mechanism of dilaton stabilization required for a viable cosmology.
  • Start/Stop/Reset: These buttons control the animation, allowing you to play, pause, and reset the simulation to its initial state.
