What is Optimal Control?

Many problems in physics and engineering involve not just understanding a system, but actively steering it to a desired state. **Optimal Control** is the mathematical field dedicated to finding the best possible way to do this. It involves three key ingredients:

  1. A **Dynamical System:** The system we want to control, governed by equations of motion. In our case, the Lunar Lander, whose state (position and velocity) is affected by gravity and engine thrust.
  2. A **Control Input:** The "knob" we can turn to influence the system. Here, it's the engine thrust level.
  3. A **Cost Function:** A measure of what we want to minimize. For the lander, the goal is to land softly (zero velocity at zero altitude) while minimizing the total fuel used.

Finding the "optimal" sequence of control inputs that minimizes the cost is a very difficult problem, often requiring complex numerical solvers.

Feedback Control: The Autopilot's Secret

Instead of calculating the entire sequence of thrusts in advance (an "open-loop" controller), it's often more robust to use a **feedback controller**. This type of controller continuously measures the system's current state and adjusts its control input in real-time to correct errors.

The "Autopilot" in this simulation uses a **PID Controller**, a workhorse of feedback control. It calculates the necessary thrust based on three terms:

  • (P)roportional: Responds to the current error. "I'm off course, I need to correct." (e.g., error in altitude)
  • (I)ntegral: Responds to the accumulated past error. "I've been off course for a while, I need to correct more strongly."
  • (D)erivative: Responds to the rate of change of the error. "I'm approaching the target too fast, I need to slow down." (e.g., velocity)

By tuning the gains of these three terms, the autopilot can achieve a smooth, stable, and highly efficient landing that is far superior to what a human can typically achieve manually.

Beginner’s Guide to the Simulation

  1. Manual Flight: Click "Reset Lander". Use the **Up Arrow** key to apply thrust. Your goal is to land on the pad (y=0) with a velocity less than 2 m/s. It's harder than it looks! You'll likely crash or run out of fuel.
  2. Engage the Autopilot: Reset the lander again, but this time, click the **"Engage Autopilot"** button. Now, watch as the feedback controller automatically adjusts the thrust to guide the lander to a perfect, soft landing.
  3. Experiment: Use the sliders to change the lander's initial altitude or the planet's gravity, then reset and engage the autopilot again. Notice how the controller adapts its behavior to the new conditions to achieve the same goal.
