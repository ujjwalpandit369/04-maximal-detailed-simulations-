Interactive Simulation: Bayesian Inference

The Framework for Updating Beliefs with Data

The Bayesian Idea: Beliefs are Probabilities

How do we learn about the world? We start with some initial beliefs, and then we update those beliefs as we gather evidence. Bayesian inference is a mathematical framework that formalizes this process. It treats "belief" as a probability distribution over a set of possible hypotheses.

In this simulation, our "hypothesis" is the value of an unknown parameter: the launch velocity of a cannon. We can't measure it directly. We can only fire the cannon and see where the ball lands. Each shot is a piece of evidence we use to refine our belief about the true velocity.

The Core Components of Bayes' Theorem

The process is governed by Bayes' Theorem: P(H|D) ∝ P(D|H) * P(H)

Which translates to: Posterior ∝ Likelihood × Prior

  • Prior (Your Initial Belief): The purple curve on the chart represents your Prior distribution. This is your belief about the cannon's velocity *before* seeing any data. We start with a wide, uncertain guess.
  • Likelihood (The Evidence): When you fire the cannon, a shot lands at a certain position. The green curve is the Likelihood. It answers the question: "If the true velocity were `v`, how likely would it be to see the shot land where it did?" It's a function that peaks at the velocity that would most likely produce the observed data.
  • Posterior (Your Updated Belief): The blue curve is the Posterior distribution. It's calculated by multiplying the Prior and the Likelihood together (and normalizing). This is your new, updated belief about the velocity *after* considering the evidence.

Notice that the Posterior is narrower than the Prior and shifted towards the peak of the Likelihood. You've learned something and become more certain! If you fire again, this Posterior becomes the new Prior for the next update.

Beginner’s Guide to the Simulation

  1. Observe the Initial State: The main chart shows your initial "Prior" belief about the cannon's velocity. It's a wide curve, meaning you're not very sure. The "True Velocity" is marked by a vertical line—your goal is to find it.
  2. Fire the Cannon: Click the "Fire Cannon & Update Belief" button. You'll see an animation of the cannon firing, and a data point (a red 'x') will appear where it landed.
  3. Watch the Update: The chart will now show three curves. The green "Likelihood" represents the information from the new data point. The simulation multiplies your old purple "Prior" by this green "Likelihood" to produce the new blue "Posterior" belief.
  4. Repeat: Click the fire button again. Your previous Posterior becomes the new Prior. Notice how with each new piece of data, your belief distribution gets narrower and more peaked around the true velocity. You are becoming more certain of the answer.
  5. Experiment: Try increasing the "Measurement Noise". This makes your data less reliable, which will be reflected in a wider Likelihood function and slower learning.
