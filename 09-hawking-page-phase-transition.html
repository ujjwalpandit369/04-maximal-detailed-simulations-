Overview

This simulation explores the remarkable thermodynamics of Anti-de Sitter (AdS) space. Unlike flat space where a black hole has negative specific heat and will evaporate, the confining "box" of AdS allows a large black hole to exist in stable thermal equilibrium. This leads to a competition between two possible states for the universe: a space filled with a uniform gas of thermal radiation, or a space containing a single, large black hole. Which state is preferred? The answer, discovered by Stephen Hawking and Don Page, depends on the temperature. This simulation calculates the Gibbs Free Energy (`F = E - TS`) for both phases. According to thermodynamic principles, the state with the lower free energy is the one that is physically realized. As you adjust the temperature, you will find a critical point, `T_c`, where the free energies cross. Below this temperature, the thermal gas is preferred. Above it, the universe undergoes a first-order phase transition, and it becomes thermodynamically favorable for the gas to collapse into a large, stable black hole. This Hawking-Page transition is not just a gravitational curiosity; via the AdS/CFT correspondence, it is dual to the confinement/deconfinement phase transition in certain quantum field theories, providing a deep link between gravity and quantum mechanics.

Core Theory

We consider a system in a fixed volume (set by the AdS radius `L`) at a temperature `T`. The stable thermodynamic state is the one that minimizes the free energy `F = E - TS`.

Phase 1: Thermal Gas in AdS

This phase consists of a thermal bath of relativistic particles. For a conformal field theory (like photons), the free energy is proportional to `-T⁴` and the volume. In `d=4` spacetime dimensions, the free energy of a thermal gas in AdS can be shown to be `F_gas = - (π²/90) g* V_eff T⁴`, where `g*` is the number of degrees of freedom. For simplicity, we can absorb the constants and state that the free energy is `F_gas = -c * (L/G) * T⁴`, where `c` is some constant. For the purpose of this simulation, we set the overall scale such that `F_gas = -T⁴`. The key feature is its monotonic decrease with temperature.

Phase 2: AdS-Schwarzschild Black Hole

A black hole in AdS has well-defined thermodynamic properties that depend on its horizon radius `r₊`.
The metric function is `f(r) = 1 - 2M/r + r²/L²`. The horizon is where `f(r₊)=0`, which gives the mass `M = (r₊/2G) * (1 + r₊²/L²)`. We set `G=1`.
Temperature: The Hawking temperature is given by the surface gravity: `T_BH = (1/4π) f'(r₊) = (1/(4πr₊)) * (1 + 3r₊²/L²)`. Note that for large `r₊`, `T_BH ~ r₊`, unlike the flat space case where `T ~ 1/r₊`. This ensures large AdS black holes have positive specific heat (`C = dE/dT > 0`) and are stable.
Entropy: The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is `S_BH = A/4G = πr₊²`.
Energy: The energy is simply the mass, `E_BH = M`.
Free Energy: `F_BH = E_BH - T_BH * S_BH = (r₊/2) * (1 - r₊²/L²)`.

The Phase Transition

The simulation plots `F_gas(T)` and `F_BH(T)`. To plot `F_BH(T)`, we must first invert the `T_BH(r₊)` relation to find `r₊(T)`. This relation is not single-valued; below a minimum temperature `T_min = sqrt(3)/(2πL)`, there are no black hole solutions. Above `T_min`, there are two branches: a small, unstable black hole (with negative specific heat) and a large, stable black hole (with positive specific heat). We are concerned with the large, stable branch. By plotting the free energies, we find they cross at a critical temperature `T_c = 3/(2πL)`. For `T < T_c`, `F_gas < F_BH`, so the gas phase dominates. For `T > T_c`, `F_BH < F_gas`, and the black hole phase dominates. This is the Hawking-Page phase transition.
