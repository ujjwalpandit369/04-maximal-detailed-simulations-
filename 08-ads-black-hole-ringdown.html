Overview

When a black hole is disturbed—for example, by a star falling into it or by merging with another black hole—it doesn't just instantly become quiet. It "rings" like a struck bell, radiating away the disturbance in the form of gravitational waves. The "sound" it produces is not arbitrary; it consists of a specific set of tones called Quasinormal Modes (QNMs). These modes are the characteristic frequencies of the black hole spacetime itself. Each QNM has a real part, corresponding to the oscillation frequency (the "pitch" of the tone), and an imaginary part, corresponding to the damping time (how quickly the tone fades away). This simulation shows this process for a scalar field perturbation. An initial pulse of energy is sent towards the black hole. You can see it interacting with the black hole's potential barrier, with some of it falling in and some being reflected. The middle panel shows the "ringdown" signal as seen by a distant observer, and the bottom panel shows the frequency spectrum of that signal. The peaks in the spectrum reveal the dominant QNM frequencies, the unique signature of the black hole's fundamental properties.

Core Theory

The evolution of a massless scalar field `Φ` in the background of a Schwarzschild black hole is described by the Klein-Gordon equation in curved spacetime, `□Φ = 0`. By decomposing the field into spherical harmonics, `Φ(t,r,θ,φ) = (1/r) Σ ψ_l(t,r) Y_lm(θ,φ)`, the problem can be reduced to a 1D wave equation for each radial part `ψ_l`:
(∂²/∂t² - ∂²/∂r*²) ψ_l(t, r*) + V_l(r) ψ_l(t, r*) = 0
This is known as the Regge-Wheeler equation. Here, `r*` is the "tortoise coordinate," defined by `dr*/dr = 1/f(r)`, where `f(r) = 1 - 2M/r`. This coordinate pushes the event horizon at `r=2M` out to `r* = -∞`. The effective potential `V_l(r)` for a scalar field is:
V_l(r) = f(r) * (l(l+1)/r² + 2M/r³) = (1 - 2M/r) * (l(l+1)/r² + 2M/r³)
This potential forms a barrier outside the black hole. An incoming wave is partially reflected by this barrier and partially transmitted to the horizon. There are no stable bound states; energy is always lost into the horizon.
A Quasinormal Mode is a solution to this wave equation that satisfies specific boundary conditions: purely ingoing waves at the horizon (`ψ ~ e⁻¹ᵎ(t+r*)`) and purely outgoing waves at infinity (`ψ ~ e⁻¹ᵎ(t-r*)`). These conditions can only be satisfied for a discrete set of complex frequencies, `ω = ω_R + iω_I`. These are the QNM frequencies. The real part `ω_R` gives the oscillation frequency, and the imaginary part `ω_I > 0` gives the exponential damping rate (`e⁻¹ᵢᵗ`). The QNM frequencies depend only on the black hole's properties (mass M, charge Q, spin J), a manifestation of the "no-hair theorem."
