The Simplest Universes: dS and AdS

After exploring spacetimes dominated by matter (black holes), we now turn to spacetimes whose properties are defined by the intrinsic energy of the vacuum itself—the cosmological constant, Lambda (Λ). The two simplest solutions to Einstein's equations with a non-zero Λ are de Sitter (dS) space for Λ > 0 and Anti-de Sitter (AdS) space for Λ < 0. Despite their simple definitions, their global properties and causal structures are profoundly different from each other and from flat space, and they serve as fundamental building blocks in modern cosmology and theoretical physics.

De Sitter Space: The Universe of Inflation

De Sitter space represents an empty, maximally symmetric universe with a positive cosmological constant. Physically, it describes an exponentially expanding spacetime. Its Penrose diagram is a simple square. The vertical edges represent spacelike infinity (i⁰), but they are identified, meaning the spatial sections are closed spheres (like the surface of the Earth). The top and bottom edges are future and past null infinity (ℐ⁺ and ℐ⁻).

The most striking feature of dS space is the presence of **cosmological event horizons**. Any observer following a timelike path (a geodesic) will have a personal event horizon. Due to the accelerated expansion, there are regions of spacetime they can never see, and other observers they can never reach, even if they were close by in the distant past. In the diagram, you can see this by drawing two worldlines for observers starting at the same point on the bottom edge (past infinity). Their future light cones do not cover the entire future boundary. This is the fundamental spacetime of cosmic inflation theory and is a good approximation for our own universe's ultimate future if dark energy is a true cosmological constant.

Anti-de Sitter Space: A Physicist's Box

Anti-de Sitter space is a maximally symmetric universe with a negative cosmological constant. Unlike dS space which expands, AdS has a kind of "gravitational pull" that contains everything within it. Its Penrose diagram is an infinite vertical strip, which is often shown as a cylinder by identifying the left and right edges. The top and bottom are future and past timelike infinity, but the crucial feature is the vertical boundary, which is a **timelike infinity**. This is radically different from flat space or dS space.

This timelike boundary acts like a perfectly reflecting box. A light ray sent from the center of AdS will travel to this boundary and reflect back to the center in a finite time. This makes the causal structure very different. There are no cosmological horizons; an observer can, in principle, receive signals from the entire spacetime. This "box-like" nature has made AdS space an incredibly fertile ground for theoretical physics, leading to the celebrated **AdS/CFT correspondence** (or holographic principle), which proposes that a theory of quantum gravity in the "bulk" of AdS is equivalent to a standard quantum field theory living on its timelike boundary.
