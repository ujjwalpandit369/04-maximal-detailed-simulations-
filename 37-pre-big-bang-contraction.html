Beginner's Guide: The Universe Before the Beginning

The Pre-Big Bang scenario offers a radical history of our universe. It proposes that before the "bang," the universe existed in a "pre-bang" phase. This was a universe that was cold, nearly empty, and infinitely large in the distant past. However, this state was unstable and began to contract. This simulation visualizes that contracting phase.

The circle on the left represents the size of our universe (its "scale factor," `a`). As the simulation runs, you see the universe shrinking. But it doesn't shrink at a constant rate; it undergoes **accelerated contraction**. It shrinks faster and faster as it approaches the "bang" at time `t=0`. The color of the background represents another key field from string theory, the "dilaton," which controls the strength of gravity. As the universe contracts, the dilaton's value grows, meaning gravity becomes incredibly strong. The plots on the right show the history of the scale factor `a(t)` (purple) and the dilaton `φ(t)` (yellow). You can clearly see `a` plummeting towards zero while `φ` races towards infinity. This phase of accelerating contraction sets the stage for the "bounce" that will create the hot, expanding universe we know.

Core Theory: The Dilaton-Driven Pre-Bang Phase

The Pre-Big Bang scenario is a cosmological model derived from the low-energy effective action of string theory. A key feature of this action is that the gravitational coupling is not a constant, but is determined by the value of a scalar field called the dilaton, `φ`. The dynamics of the universe are governed by the coupled equations for the scale factor `a(t)` and the dilaton `φ(t)`.

The model proposes that the universe begins (`t -> -∞`) in a state of "string perturbative vacuum." This is a state that is cold, empty, flat, and, crucially, has very weak gravitational coupling (the dilaton `φ -> -∞`). This state is, however, unstable to quantum fluctuations. The universe begins to contract, and the dilaton begins to evolve. The equations of motion sourced by the kinetic energy of the dilaton and the curvature lead to a period of "super-inflationary" contraction (also called hyper-inflation). For time `t` approaching zero from the negative side, the solutions are:
a(t) ∝ (-t)^(-1/2)
φ(t) = -√(3) * log(-t) + const.
The Hubble parameter `H = (da/dt)/a = -1/(2t)` is negative (contraction) and its magnitude is growing. This is a phase of accelerated contraction.

As `t -> 0`, the scale factor `a -> 0` and the dilaton `φ -> +∞`. This means the curvature of spacetime diverges, and the gravitational coupling `g_s ~ exp(φ)` also diverges. The universe approaches a singularity. In classical General Relativity, this is the end of the story. However, in string theory, this is where the T-duality symmetry (visualized in the previous simulation) is expected to become important. The `a -> 0` singularity is supposed to be resolved by new stringy physics, leading to a "bounce" or "graceful exit" into the hot, expanding, post-big bang phase of standard cosmology. This simulation visualizes the pre-bounce phase, showing the runaway contraction and the simultaneous strengthening of gravity.
